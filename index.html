For the first-timer

Teka's survival guide

Teka has never been to the island and doesn't speak much Spanish yet. Here's the essential stuff so the culture makes sense — and so he doesn't miss the good parts.

🍺

The colmado

The neighborhood's corner-store-meets-bar-meets-social-hub. You order one big Presidente jumbo (the large bottle) — ice-cold and frosted, what locals call "vestida de novia" ("dressed like a bride") — and pour it into little cups to share. Loud music, plastic chairs on the sidewalk, everyone talking. The most authentic thing you'll do all week.

🎺

Grupo Bonyé

Every Sunday evening they play live son and merengue among the ruins of the San Francisco Monastery in the Colonial Zone. Free, open-air, packed with Dominicans dancing. Get there early (~5 pm), grab a beer, and let it happen. Non-negotiable.

🍽️

What to eat & drink

La bandera (rice, beans, meat — the national plate), mangú (mashed plantains) for breakfast, tostones, fried fish on the beach, chicharrón. To drink: Presidente, Brugal / Barceló rum, mamajuana, and fresh juices (passion fruit, papaya, morir soñando).

💃

Merengue & bachata

Music here isn't background — it's the language. Nobody expects Teka to dance well; just move and laugh. If a doña pulls you up to dance, the answer is yes. Merengue is fast; bachata is slow and romantic.

🌞

Island time

"Ahora" (now) can mean in a while. "Ahorita" can mean even later. Things happen at their own pace — that's part of the charm. It's hot and humid: water, a hat, and shade at midday.

🤝

People & tipping

Dominicans are warm and chatty. Greet everyone with "buenas." Restaurants usually add ~10% service; tip a bit more on top. Agree taxi and market prices upfront. Carry cash in pesos for colmados and guaguas (buses).

Logistics

Before you go

What's worth sorting out ahead of time and on the ground.

🛂

Documents

Valid passport. This is an international flight → proof of citizenship required. Fill out the DR e-Ticket online (entry and exit) before each flight.

💵

Money

Currency: Dominican peso (RD$). USD is accepted in many tourist spots, but carry pesos in cash for colmados, buses and markets. Cards work at hotels and bigger restaurants.

📱

Connectivity

Get an eSIM (Claro/Altice) or use roaming. WhatsApp runs everything here — taxis, tours, bookings. Uber works in Santo Domingo.

🚐

Getting around

To the north: Caribe Tours (comfortable, cheap express bus) or a private driver. In cities: Uber in SD, agreed-price taxis, and guaguas (local minibuses) for the adventurous.

🌡️

June weather

Hot and humid (30–33°C / 86–91°F), strong sun, short afternoon showers. Pack light clothes, swimsuit, sunscreen, bug spray, a cap, and a thin rain layer.

🛟

Safety

Normal city sense: no flashy jewelry at night, take a taxi/Uber when out late, carry just enough cash. The Colonial Zone and tourist areas are calm and walkable.

The key logistics point — the Wednesday return The flight leaves from SDQ (Santo Domingo) at 5:17 pm, but Wednesday we wake up on the north coast. The drive back is ~3.5–4 hrs from Puerto Plata (Route A) or ~2.5–3 hrs from Samaná (Route B). Either way, leave the north by 9–10 am at the latest (a private driver gives more flexibility than the bus) to reach the airport with a 2.5–3 hour buffer. If easy timing matters most, Route B (Samaná) is the safer bet.
